Types of Inspections

A property inspection requires comprehensive knowledge of building systems and components which are always evolving. Home inspectors must have a significant range of knowledge and skills in order to be capable of providing a thorough inspection. The inspection is a non-invasive observation and examination of the systems, structures, and components of the home. It is to identify “wear and tear” and provide maintenance recommendations, identify material defects such as improper installations, and even identify systems or components that are outdated based on modern building standards. Due to the wide range of building construction practices, a professional home inspector can provide a wealth of information to you.

General Property (Home) Inspection - Comprehensive top-to-bottom evaluations evaluating structural stability, roofing assemblies, electrical systems, and core utility infrastructure.

Roof Inspection - An examination of the roofing systems, drainage systems, and overall condition. It focuses on identifying missing, damaged, or aging components, as well as material defects. Additionally, the inspection looks for signs of sagging, water ponding, or structural weakness from both the exterior and, when accessible, the interior attic space to check for active leaks or wood rot.

San Francisco (RECO) Energy & Water Inspection - A mandatory compliance evaluation required for residential properties built before 1978 prior to a transfer of title, property sale, or a major remodeling project. The San Francisco Residential Energy and Water Ordinance inspection ensures compliance with municipal energy and water conservation standards under the San Francisco Housing Code. During the on-site property evaluation, a certified inspector thoroughly reviews energy efficiency items—including hot water heater insulation, weather-stripping on exterior doors, insulation on accessible HVAC ducts, attic insulation, and the caulking or sealing of major building joints and openings. The water conservation assessment covers plumbing fixtures, verifying flow rates for toilets (1.28 gallons per flush), showerheads (1.8 gallons per minute), and faucet aerators (1.8 gallons per minute), alongside a complete visual check to confirm the absence of plumbing leaks. For seamless point-of-sale clearance, our certified inspection company also reviews essential residential safety components, verifying water heater seismic strapping alongside operational smoke alarms and carbon monoxide detectors. Following a passing evaluation, the certified inspector handles the filing with the San Francisco Department of Building Inspection (DBI) and recording with the San Francisco Assessor's Office.
